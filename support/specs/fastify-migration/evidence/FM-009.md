### Contract details

- Factory declaration inspection and concrete input binding run without executing
  factories. Composition validates names, explicit replacements, exposure lists,
  bindings, module prerequisites, and controller/action targets offline.
- Inputs remain factory-specific; heterogeneous arrays accept
  `ControllerRegistration extends ExtensionFactory<never, object>`. Authors do
  not cast or receive a service locator. `requires` validates module names only.
  The sole dynamic action-signature assertion is in the internal `bindAction`
  resolver after callability validation. No `any` annotations or casts to `any`
  are used.
- Internal composition consumes registrations in previously resolved low-to-high
  provider order. Full API2 loading/provider ordering remains FM-017; this task
  does not reinterpret or reject the current production API1 declarations.
- Selection precedes construction. Bindings identify exact descriptor objects,
  rejecting unknown/same-name-but-different/duplicate/missing bindings. Bindings
  for losing declarations may be present or absent; their factories never run.
- Replacement must preserve the action allowlist. Implementation selection has
  no mechanism to modify route access, CSRF, validation, or policy metadata.
- `instantiate()` belongs after application initialization and before Fastify
  ready/listen, per design §10.1. It constructs each winner once, validates every
  declared method even without a targeting route, resolves deliberate inherited
  methods, and binds actions to the selected instance. Results and failures are
  retained; repeated preparation cannot reconstruct a partial instance set.
- Missing/non-callable instance methods fail at preparation, not offline by
  prematurely executing factories. Forbidden prototype/constructor targets,
  duplicate names and unlisted targets fail offline. Action accessors are rejected
  without invocation; helpers are not discovered/exposed. Invalid/asynchronous
  factory results fail before readiness, including empty exposure lists.
- Each composition owns its own controller instances; constructors wire borrowed
  operations and acquire no resources. Route installation/lifecycle/security
  composition are later tasks, not hidden behavior of registration.
